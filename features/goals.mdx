Goals in Life OS are not a rigid target system — they are a way of staying connected to what you are actually working towards. A goal captures a direction, a reason, and a next step, so that every day you have a clear answer to "what am I moving toward and what can I do about it today?" The system is intentionally lightweight: you do not need a full project plan, a deadline, or a perfect strategy. You just need a name, a why, and the very next thing you can do.

## Creating a Goal

<Steps>
  <Step title="Give it a name">
    Open the **Goals** section from the sidebar and tap **New Goal**. Write a name that describes an outcome you want, not a task. "Run a 5K" or "Build a freelance client base" work well. "Go running more" is too vague to feel satisfying when complete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign it to a Life Area">
    Link the goal to one of your Life Areas — for example, Health, Career, or Relationships. This keeps your goals organised and makes it easy to see how your ambitions are distributed across your life. A goal without a Life Area is allowed, but assigning one helps you spot imbalance over time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a target date (optional)">
    If the goal has a natural deadline — a race, a launch date, a season — tap **Target date** and choose one. If it is open-ended, leave this blank. Life OS will not nag you about missing a date; it is a reference point, not a countdown.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your 'why' statement">
    Tap **Why does this matter?** and write one or two sentences about what achieving this goal would mean for you. This step is easy to skip and worth doing anyway. On the days when motivation is low, your own words about why this matters are more powerful than any reminder notification.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Adding Next Steps

A goal by itself is a wish. A goal with a next step is a plan.

After creating a goal, open it and tap **Add next step**. Write the single, concrete action that would move the goal forward right now — not the full list of everything required, just the next move. Examples:

- Goal: "Learn conversational Spanish" → Next step: "Download the Pimsleur app and complete lesson one"
- Goal: "Improve my fitness" → Next step: "Book a gym induction for this week"
- Goal: "Write a personal essay" → Next step: "Spend 20 minutes free-writing about the memory from 2019"

Once you complete a next step, Life OS prompts you to add the following one. This keeps your goals alive and your Today view fresh without requiring you to think about planning every time you sit down to work.

<Accordion title="Can I add multiple next steps at once?">
  Yes. If you want to map out several steps ahead, tap **Add next step** multiple times. Life OS will surface them in order, one at a time, in your Today view. Only the first incomplete next step is shown in Today — this is intentional, to prevent the list from becoming a source of anxiety.
</Accordion>

## Goal Progress

Progress on a goal is shown as a percentage calculated from the ratio of completed next steps to total next steps added so far. You will see this as a progress bar on the goal card.

To **mark a goal complete**, open the goal and tap **Mark as complete** at the bottom of the card. You will be invited to write a short completion note — what you learned, how it felt, or what you want to remember. This note is saved to your Reflect archive.

<Tip>
  Keep your active goals list to three to five at a time. More than that and your attention fragments — every goal gets a little, none gets enough. If you have a long list of things you want to do, park the lower-priority ones as paused goals and return to them when space opens up.
</Tip>

## Pausing and Archiving Goals

Life changes. Goals that made sense in January might not belong in your Today view in April.

**To pause a goal**, open it and tap **Pause**. A paused goal disappears from Today and from your active list, but all its data — steps, progress, your why statement — is preserved exactly as you left it. Resume it at any time from the **Paused** section of the Goals page.

**To archive a goal**, open it and tap **Archive**. Use this for goals you have completed, or for directions you have genuinely decided not to pursue. Archived goals are read-only and stored permanently under **Archive** in the Goals section. You can always revisit them, but they will not appear in any active views.

<Warning>
  **Goals and Projects are different things.** A Goal is a direction with an outcome and a next step — it lives in this section. A [Project](/system/projects) is a structured body of work with multiple tasks, subtasks, and milestones, organised under a Life Area. If you find yourself adding ten next steps to a goal and building a dependency chain, it is a sign you are actually describing a project. Move the work to Projects and keep this goal as the outcome it is pointing toward.
</Warning>
