Life OS is not a collection of features bolted together. Every part of the system is designed to feed into the next, forming a deliberate loop that keeps your intentions connected to your actions — and your actions connected back to what you actually want. Once you see the loop, everything in Life OS starts to make sense.

## The Four Phases

The loop has four phases. Each one does a distinct job, and each one depends on the others to work properly.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Plan" icon="bullseye">
    **Goals + Life Areas.** Decide what matters and choose your direction. This is where you name the parts of life important to you, set goals that point toward where you want to go, and break those goals into concrete steps. Without a plan, everything else is just activity.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Act" icon="bolt">
    **Today + Schedule.** Take action each day on what matters. Today surfaces your priorities for the day. Schedule keeps your commitments visible across days, weeks, and months. This is where intention meets execution.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Build" icon="repeat">
    **Habits.** Compound small daily actions into lasting change. Habits encode your values into your routine so that the most important behaviours happen consistently — not through willpower, but through structure. Over time, small daily actions become who you are.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Learn" icon="magnifying-glass">
    **Reflect.** Close the loop, adjust, and improve. The weekly review is where you look back honestly, notice what's working, and decide what to change. Without this phase, the other three gradually drift out of alignment.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Why the Loop Matters

Each phase on its own has real limitations. Planning without action is wishful thinking — you can spend hours designing a perfect system and never change anything. Action without planning is scattered — you stay busy, but you're not necessarily moving toward anything that matters to you. Habits without reflection can calcify into routines that no longer serve you. And reflection without a plan to return to produces insight that evaporates by Monday morning.

The loop is what makes each piece useful. When you plan, you give your daily actions a target. When you act, you test whether your plan was realistic. When you build habits, you stop relying on daily decisions for the things that should be automatic. And when you reflect, you use everything you've learned to make the next cycle of the loop better than the last.

This is how progress compounds. Not through one big push, but through many small, connected cycles.

## Getting Into the Loop

You don't need a perfect setup to start. The loop works at any scale, and the fastest way to understand it is to run one cycle — however small.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick one Life Area">
    Choose one area of your life that feels most important or most neglected right now. That's your starting point.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set one Goal">
    Within that life area, choose one goal — something concrete and meaningful that you want to move toward in the next month or quarter. Break it into a few steps.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add one Habit">
    Identify the single daily action that would most reliably move you toward that goal. Add it to your habits and aim for consistency, not perfection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Today daily">
    Each morning, open Today and choose your top priorities. Let your goal and habit appear here — alongside everything else that's on your plate — so you always know what matters most before the day pulls you elsewhere.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do one weekly review">
    At the end of the week, open Reflect and answer the prompts honestly. Did you do what you planned? What got in the way? What would you do differently? Then use those answers to adjust your plan for next week.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That's one full cycle of the loop. It probably won't be smooth. You'll miss days, your goal might need adjusting, and your reflection will probably surface things you didn't expect. That's the point — the loop is a learning machine, not a performance standard.

Once you've run one cycle, you'll know exactly how to improve the next one. Then you can add more life areas, more goals, and more habits — gradually building a system that's genuinely shaped around your life.

<Tip>
  You don't need everything configured before the loop becomes useful. One imperfect cycle — a rough plan, a single habit, and one honest reflection — will teach you more about how to use Life OS than a perfect setup that never gets tested. Start small. Start now.
</Tip>
