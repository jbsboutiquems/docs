title: "Projects: Plan and Complete Structured, Time-Bound Work"

---

Goals give you direction; projects give you traction. A goal like "get stronger" is a horizon you keep moving toward — it doesn't have a finish line. A project like "complete a 5k in under 30 minutes" is a structured, time-bound effort with a clear outcome you can declare done. In Life OS, projects are where intentional direction becomes concrete, coordinated action — and every project lives inside a Life Area, so your work always stays connected to the part of life it's actually serving.

## Creating a Project

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Projects">
    From the sidebar, tap **Projects**, then hit **+ New Project** in the top-right corner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it clearly">
    Give your project a name that describes the outcome, not just the activity. "Launch personal website" is better than "Website stuff" — you'll know when it's done.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign it to a Life Area">
    Choose the Life Area this project belongs to. Every project needs a home. This connects it to your broader intentions and surfaces it in the Life Areas overview.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set an optional deadline">
    If there's a date by which this needs to be done — or a date by which you *want* it done — add it here. Deadlines are optional but help with prioritisation and weekly planning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a definition of done">
    In one sentence, describe what "finished" looks like. This is the single most useful thing you can do when starting a project. "Done means the site is live, has a contact form, and I've shared it with three people." Ambiguity is the enemy of follow-through.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Tap **Save**. Your project is created with **Active** status and is ready for tasks.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Adding Tasks to a Project

Tasks are the individual steps that move a project forward. Keep them small enough to complete in one sitting — if a task feels too big, break it down further.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your project">
    Tap any project from the Projects list to open its detail view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a task">
    Tap **+ Add Task** inside the project. Give it a clear, action-oriented name — start with a verb ("Write intro copy", "Book the venue", "Send draft to reviewer").
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a due date (optional)">
    If a specific task needs to happen by a certain date, add a due date. Tasks with due dates will appear in your Schedule and Today views at the right time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark tasks complete">
    Tap the checkbox next to any task to mark it done. Completed tasks move to the **Completed** section at the bottom of the project so you can see your progress accumulate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep your active project list to **3–5 projects at most**. A longer list isn't ambition — it's a recipe for paralysis. If you have more than five active projects, move some to **On Hold** until you have genuine bandwidth for them.
</Tip>

## Project Status

Every project carries one of four statuses. Use them honestly — they're for your own clarity, not for appearances.

| Status | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Active** | You're working on this now. Tasks are being added and completed on a regular basis. |
| **On Hold** | Real life intervened, or you've consciously deprioritised this. Nothing is happening, but you intend to return to it. |
| **Completed** | Your definition of done has been met. Mark it complete and feel good about it. |
| **Archived** | You're letting this go — circumstances changed, the goal shifted, or it no longer makes sense. Archiving keeps your list clean without erasing the history. |

To change a project's status, open the project and tap the status badge at the top of the detail view. Completed and Archived projects are hidden from the default Projects list but remain accessible via the **All Projects** filter.

## Projects vs. Goals

It's easy to confuse projects and goals — both involve ambition and forward movement. Here's the clearest way to think about the distinction:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Goals">
    A goal is a **long-horizon direction** — something you're always moving toward rather than finishing. It's measured by progress, momentum, and consistency, not by a binary done/not-done state.

    - **Timeframe:** Ongoing. No natural end date.
    - **Measured by:** Progress indicators, habit streaks, directional movement.
    - **Example:** *"Get stronger"* — you'll always be working on this. You don't "finish" being strong.
    - **Lives in:** Goals tracker, assigned to a Life Area.
    - **Question it answers:** *"What direction am I heading?"*
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Projects">
    A project is a **time-bound, structured effort** with a defined outcome you can declare complete. When the definition of done is met, the project is finished — even if the underlying goal continues.

    - **Timeframe:** Bounded. Has (or implies) an end.
    - **Measured by:** Task completion, deliverables, a clear done state.
    - **Example:** *"Complete a 5k in under 30 minutes"* — when you cross the finish line under time, this is done.
    - **Lives in:** Projects, assigned to a Life Area and optionally linked to a goal.
    - **Question it answers:** *"What am I building or doing right now?"*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  A single goal can inspire many projects over time. "Get stronger" might lead to a "Start a gym routine" project this quarter, a "Complete a 5k" project next quarter, and a "Learn to rock climb" project the year after. Projects are how you make progress on goals in concrete, completable chunks.
</Note>
