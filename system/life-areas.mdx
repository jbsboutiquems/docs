Life Areas are the top-level organiser in Life OS. Everything you care about — your goals, your habits, your projects — lives under one of the domains of your life that matter most to you. Rather than working from an undifferentiated pile of tasks and intentions, Life Areas give your efforts a home, so you always know *why* something is on your plate and *where* it belongs in the larger picture of who you're becoming.

## What Is a Life Area?

A Life Area is a named, sustained domain of your life — the kind of thing you'll always be tending, not finishing. **Health**, **Career**, **Relationships**, **Finances**, **Personal Growth**, **Creative & Fun** — these aren't projects you complete or tasks you check off. They're the ongoing territories of a well-lived life, each deserving regular attention and intention.

Think of a Life Area as the answer to the question: *"What part of my life does this belong to?"* When you capture a new goal or build a new habit, tagging it to a Life Area grounds it in meaning and makes it easier to stay balanced across everything that matters.

<Note>
  A Life Area is **not** a project (which has a defined end) and **not** a task (which has a single action). It's a sustained area of responsibility — something you steward over the long term.
</Note>

## Creating Life Areas

You can create as many Life Areas as you need, and personalise each one so it feels like yours.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Life Areas">
    From the sidebar, tap **Life Areas**. On your first visit you'll see a prompt to add your first area.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap 'Add Life Area'">
    Hit the **+ Add Life Area** button in the top-right corner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name your area">
    Give it a name that resonates with you. "Health & Fitness", "Work", "Money" — use language that feels natural, not corporate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a colour (optional)">
    Pick a colour to visually distinguish this area across the app. Colour-coded labels appear on goals, habits, and projects linked to this area.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add an icon (optional)">
    Select an emoji or icon to represent the area. This shows up in the Life Areas overview and in the Today view, making it easy to scan at a glance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Tap **Save**. Your new Life Area is ready to receive goals, habits, and projects.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Assigning Content to Life Areas

Once your Life Areas exist, everything else in Life OS connects back to them.

- **Goals** — when you create or edit a goal, choose a Life Area from the dropdown. The goal will appear in that area's summary view and count toward its activity.
- **Habits** — assign each habit to a Life Area so your routines are grounded in a clear domain (e.g. a "30-minute walk" habit lives under Health & Fitness).
- **Projects** — every project belongs to exactly one Life Area. This keeps structured work tethered to the part of your life it's actually serving.

The **Life Areas overview page** shows a summary card for each area, displaying active goals, active habits, and open projects at a glance. Think of it as a bird's-eye health check for your whole life — open it weekly to see where energy is flowing and where it isn't.

## Rebalancing Your Life

One of the most powerful things about Life Areas is what they reveal when you step back and look at the full picture. If every active goal points to **Career** and nothing is assigned to **Relationships** or **Health**, that's not an accident — it's a pattern worth examining.

Use the Life Areas overview as a regular rebalancing tool:

- Are any areas empty? That's a signal, not a failure — decide consciously whether to invest there or let it rest for now.
- Are any areas overloaded? Too many goals and projects in one domain can mean you're avoiding other parts of life, or simply overcommitted in one place.
- Do your habits reflect the areas you say matter most? If Personal Growth is a priority but you have no habits there, the system will show you that gap gently.

The goal isn't perfect symmetry — it's intentional choice. Life OS helps you see the full picture so you can decide, on your own terms, where to put your energy.

## Starter Life Areas

Not sure where to begin? Here are six areas that cover most people's lives well. Use them as-is, rename them, or mix and match.

<Accordion title="Health & Fitness">
  Everything related to your physical body and energy: exercise, sleep, nutrition, medical care, and recovery. Habits like daily movement or a consistent bedtime live here. Goals like "improve my resting heart rate" or "run a 10k" belong here too.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Career & Work">
  Your professional life, whether that means a job, a business, a freelance practice, or a creative career. Goals around growth, skills, income, and impact. Projects tied to specific professional outcomes.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Relationships">
  The people who matter — partners, family, close friends, community. Habits like regular check-in calls or date nights, and goals around being more present, deepening friendships, or showing up better for the people you love.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Finances">
  Your financial life: budgeting, saving, investing, debt reduction, and building security. Goals like "build a 3-month emergency fund" or "pay off my credit card" live here, alongside habits like reviewing your budget weekly.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Personal Growth">
  Learning, self-awareness, therapy, reading, spiritual practice, and anything else oriented toward becoming more fully yourself. Habits like journaling or meditation. Goals around skills you're developing or ways you want to grow.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Creative & Fun">
  The things you do because you love them — hobbies, art, music, travel, play. This area is often the first to disappear when life gets busy. Keeping it visible reminds you that rest and joy are not optional extras.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  Aim for **4–7 Life Areas**. Fewer than four can mean you're not capturing the full picture; more than seven starts to fragment your focus and make the overview feel overwhelming. The sweet spot is a set of areas broad enough to be meaningful but distinct enough to be useful.
</Tip>
