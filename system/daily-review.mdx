Your brain is not a great place to store unfinished business. Every open loop — the task you didn't get to, the thing you meant to log, tomorrow's looming priority — stays active in your mind even after you close your laptop. The Daily Review is a short, intentional ritual designed to close those loops before you rest. It takes five minutes. It makes the next day calmer. And over time, the small daily entries it generates become the raw material for a much clearer view of how your life is actually going.

## Starting the Daily Review

You can open the Daily Review from two places:

- **From Today:** At the end of the day, a **Daily Review** prompt appears at the bottom of your Today view. Tap it to begin.
- **From Reflect:** Navigate to **Reflect** in the sidebar, then tap **Daily Review**. This works at any time of day — useful if you're doing a catch-up review in the morning for the previous day.

<Note>
  You can also set a daily reminder to nudge you at a consistent time each evening. Go to **Settings → Notifications → Daily Review Reminder** to turn this on and choose your preferred time.
</Note>

## The Review Flow

The Daily Review moves through five steps. Each one is designed to be fast — a sentence or two is enough.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Catch up on completions">
    Scroll through your habits and goal steps for the day. Check off anything you completed but didn't mark in the moment — a walk you took, a reading session you squeezed in. The log should reflect what actually happened, not just what you remembered to tap.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note one win">
    Name one thing that went well today. It doesn't need to be significant — a good conversation, a task you finally tackled, a moment of focus in a scattered day. Writing it down trains you to notice what's working, not just what's left undone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Capture what's lingering">
    Is anything still rattling around in your head? A task you didn't finish, something you need to follow up on, a vague worry? Write it down. The act of capturing it tells your brain it's been seen and doesn't need to keep flagging it. If it's actionable, it'll surface in tomorrow's planning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your top priority for tomorrow">
    Name the single most important thing you want to accomplish tomorrow. Just one. This becomes the anchor that shapes your Today view when you open Life OS in the morning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the day">
    Tap **Close the Day**. Your entries are saved, your streaks are updated, and your daily review is logged. You're done. Put the phone down.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How the Daily Review Feeds the Weekly Review

No daily entry exists in isolation. Every win you log, every lingering item you capture, every top priority you set — all of it accumulates quietly in the background.

When you open your **Weekly Review** at the end of the week, Life OS surfaces your daily entries as a ready-made record of how the week actually unfolded. Instead of trying to reconstruct seven days from memory, you'll have a trail of small honest observations to reflect on. Patterns become visible: which days were energising, which felt scattered, whether your stated priorities matched how you actually spent your time.

The daily review doesn't require depth. That's what the weekly review is for. Think of daily entries as seeds — brief, honest, regular — and the weekly review as the harvest.

<Tip>
  Keep the Daily Review to **five minutes**. If you find yourself writing paragraphs, you're doing the weekly review's job. Save the depth and reflection for your weekly session — the daily review is about closing loops, not processing them.
</Tip>

<Note>
  For the full weekly reflection experience — reviewing your life areas, assessing progress on goals, and setting intentions for the week ahead — see the [Reflect feature guide](/features/reflect).
</Note>
